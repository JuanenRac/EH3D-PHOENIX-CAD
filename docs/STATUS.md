# EH3D-PHOENIX-CAD: status

## Current state

EH3D-PHOENIX-CAD is early code. Beside the base that every repository of the suite carries (manifest, README files in eight languages, changelog, CI, shared checks) and the written scope, requirements, dependencies, threat model and decisions, it has a Rust library, `eh3d-phoenix-cad`: lengths in integer nanometres with their own range, a parametric enclosure with round, countersunk, counterbored, rectangular and oval holes in its faces and rounded or chamfered edges, structured change operations that report conflicts and leave the model unchanged (each with an exact inverse), an OpenSCAD-style script, and a fit check of the envelope of a board. It has only been tried on models made by its own tests. It also writes the enclosure as a STEP file (a boundary representation with the inner space as a void and a cylindrical face for each hole), the same text for the same model; that file has only been checked for its structure (header, references, counts) and no CAD program has opened it, and it draws only the box with straight round holes: a model with rounded or chamfered edges or another kind of hole is refused by `export_step`, while the script draws them all. Around it the library has integer angles with an integer sine and cosine, units of length read and shown in mm, um, in and mil, stable names for faces, edges and hole walls, a recipe of steps with a tip, suppressed steps and an undo history, a settings file with a printer profile, a table of screws and a table of densities, atomic saving with numbered copies, the volume, the mass, the centre of mass and the moments of inertia, patterns of holes, a graph of dependencies with a recalculation that only does what changed, named variables and variants, parameters that are values, variables or expressions (a small language with units, functions and conditions over exact fractions, whose limits are in the settings) calculated through the graph of dependencies, reference frames, the envelope of a project file, and the text of every diagnostic in eight languages. The box also has posts that rise from the floor under the mounting holes of a board (a bore for a threaded insert or a self-tapping screw taken from the table of the settings, an optional conical foot, checks against the walls, the other posts, the holes of the floor and the height of the board and its tallest part) and a lid that is screwed into them (a through hole with a recess for the head over each post, or over the four corners when there are no posts, and an optional centring lip with the clearance of the printer profile); the script draws all of it and the STEP file draws the posts with a plain bore. The project file keeps the recipe of the box in its mechanical domain (the parameters, the ordered steps, the tip and the interface of the board), read again to the same box, with a migration from the older domain of the parameters; the contract is in `docs/PROJECT_DOMAIN.md`. The 2D sketch (`docs/SKETCH.md`) holds points, lines, circles, arcs, polylines and rectangles on a face of the box, geometric and dimensional constraints (a dimension can follow a variable), a least-squares solver that reports the degrees of freedom left, the constraints that repeat or contradict each other, or that it did not converge, kept in the project file (a domain `sketch` of its own, optional and with its own version, so that a project without sketches is the file it always was) with the sketch file `.phoenixsketch` as a way in and a way out, and a window. The closed outlines of a sketch (chains of lines and arcs and circles, with the holes found by containment) can be raised from a face (`Extrude`) or cut into it (`Pocket`, to a depth or through) as steps of the recipe, which then needs version 2 of the mechanical domain; the sketch has only been tried on sketches made by its own tests. A hole or a post can be repeated as one step of the recipe, along a line or round a centre (a pattern: each position is a hole or a post of its own, checked like one added by hand, either all are accepted or none, and editing the pattern builds the positions again), and a set of cooling fins can rise from a face (how many, how thick, how tall, how far apart and where they run), checked against the edges and the holes of the face and the other sets; both are steps of version 3 of the mechanical domain. The cooling fins come with an estimate of the heat they give to still air, worked out from a coefficient of natural convection, a conductivity and a temperature of the settings; it is the estimate of a simple model, not a measurement and not a simulation, and it has been compared with no reference. There is no server task, and nothing has been run on a machine. A catalog of parts (`docs/CATALOG.md`) reads libraries of profiles, screws and angle brackets from plain text files, places them in the model as steps of the recipe (the position, one of the 24 turns that keep the axes on the axes and, for a profile, the length it is cut to; the recipe is then written as version 4 of the mechanical domain, and only then), refuses a cut outside the bar, a part too far from the origin and two profiles that share volume, and adds the parts up in a bill of materials and a cut list with the volume and the mass for the density of each material. It has only been tried on parts made up for its examples and tests, and no part has been compared with a real one. Simple assemblies (`docs/ASSEMBLY.md`) place one part relative to another along one axis with mates (a gap, an alignment by the lower face, the upper face or the middle, or a stack; the position typed for a part stays in the model and the mates are applied on top of it; the recipe is then written as version 5 of the mechanical domain, and only then) and find the pairs of parts that share volume, with the exact shapes of profiles, brackets and screws in whole nanometres, and a screw through the hole of a bracket allowed when its shank fits; the report, the tree, the fields, the colours of the views and the command `interference` show them. They have only been tried on the two example projects and on hand-made cases. The solid of the model can be written as an STL file, binary or text (`solid.rs`, `mesh.rs`): the box with its holes of every style, posts, lip, fins, the extrusions and pockets of the sketches and every placed part, as closed surfaces whose volume is checked against the exact volume of the model; a model with a shape the solid cannot be made of is refused with `PHOENIX-CAD-048` (`EXPORT.md` lists them). The plan, the front and the side are written as DXF and SVG drawings (`drawing.rs`) with the layers `CONTORNO`, `AGUJEROS` and `EJES`. None of these files has been opened in a slicer, a CAD program or a drawing program.

## The window

The program has a window (egui, built with `--features gui`): a tree of the model, a panel of properties where a dimension (a length, a variable or a formula) or the value of a hole, a post, an edge treatment or the lid is edited and the box is built again, a 3D view and a plan drawn as lines (a hole or a post is picked and dragged with the mouse, snapped to a grid of the settings and kept only where the model accepts it, as one entry of the history; distances between points and edges and angles between edges or at a vertex are measured and read in the status bar and in a panel, in the unit of the settings), undo and redo, a sheet of variables (a value or a formula for each, what it calculates to and the parameters that use it) and buttons that add a hole, a post, the edge treatment, an extrusion or a pocket and remove a step, and a status bar with the sizes, the fit of the board and the steps the model refused, and an Export menu that writes the script, the STEP file or the text of the fit report to a file (the name suggested follows the project; nothing is written over a file that exists unless a box in the dialog is ticked, the file of the project that is open and names that belong to a project or a sketch are always refused, and the status bar gives the code of the reason: `PHOENIX-CAD-040` for a file that exists, `PHOENIX-CAD-041` for the other refusals, `PHOENIX-CAD-022` when the STEP file does not draw a shape of the model, such as the extrusions and pockets of the sketches), and a window that shows the fit report as text to select or copy. There is no STL or DXF file, because the library draws no mesh and no flat view. The state of the window, the edits, the history, the views and the texts in the eight languages are code of the library that is tested without a screen. The drawing code of the window itself has been written against the interface of the library and has never been opened on a screen: its look, its layout and its behaviour with the mouse are not checked by any test (what the mouse does is decided in the library: which hole or post is under the pointer, where on its plane the pointer is, the snapping, the refusal of a position and the measurements are tested without a screen). The window has a second part for the 2D sketch (`docs/SKETCH.md`): a canvas with a grid, tools to draw points, lines, polylines, rectangles, circles and arcs, snaps, constraints and dimensions added from the selection (a dimension can be a value, a variable or a formula), dragging with the solver running at each move, an indicator of the degrees of freedom that is green, amber or red with the list of the constraints that repeat or contradict the others, undo and redo, the sketch file and a button that keeps the sketch in the project under a name. Its state is also code of the library tested without a screen, and its drawing code has never been opened on a screen either. The `check` command reads a project, with its sketches, and prints the same measures and the state of every step; it takes the settings of the sketches from the file given with `--config` too. A pattern of holes, a pattern of posts and a set of cooling fins are added from the window with buttons of their own, show in the tree, and are edited from the properties (the whole pattern is built again at each change, and a position that the model refuses leaves everything as it was); the properties of the fins show their surface and the estimate of their heat, marked as an estimate, and the report lists the same estimate for each set. A library of parts is opened from a button and its parts are listed in the tree, with their size, volume and mass; a part is added at the place the settings say, moving along to a free place, and its position, direction, roll and cut are edited like any other value (a refusal is told with its code and leaves the part as it was); the report gives the totals, a line for each part and cut, and the cut list of each profile, and the bill of materials is written as comma separated values from the Export menu, where the STL file and the DXF and SVG drawings of the three views are written too (the command `export` does the same from the command line).

## Capabilities

The official state of each capability is in REQUIREMENTS.md: 8 requirements: 7 `prototype` (FCAD-001, FCAD-003, FCAD-004, FCAD-005, FCAD-006, FCAD-007, FCAD-008) and 1 `planned` (FCAD-002). This page never states a state that REQUIREMENTS.md does not.

## Open questions

* Which geometry kernel to use for solid operations beyond the box. Candidates being looked at: a Rust solid-modelling crate with STEP support and OpenCASCADE through bindings (not verified). Licences must be compatible with GPL-3.0-or-later. The constraint solver of the sketch is not an open question: it is written in this repository (damped least squares, no dependency).
* Where the 3D board view and the enclosure fit check live: here or in the board tool.

## Known technical debt and omitted tests

The holes and the posts carry the shared object identity (UUID v7 of EH3D-COMMON). The mechanical domain of the project format is written and read by this repository only: the board tool does not read the interface of the board or write steps yet, and no real project has gone through it. The posts and the lid have been tried on boxes and boards made by their own tests; nothing has been printed, and the bores of the table of posts were typed by hand and are not compared with any insert or screw. The script with posts and lid has not been opened in OpenSCAD, and the STEP file with posts has been checked for its structure only. The mass and the moments leave the posts, the lip and the holes of the lid out and say so. The language of expressions has only been tried against hand-made tables and generated expressions, never inside a design; its sine and cosine come from the integer table of angles (nine decimals), and it has no tangent, no area or volume, and no functions defined by the person. The script has not been opened in OpenSCAD and nothing is compared with a reference CAD program. The table of screws and the table of densities in the settings file were typed by hand and are not compared with any standard. The volume of a hole is exact only when the hole lies in the thin part of its face; otherwise the figure says it is not. The centre of mass and the moments of inertia are checked against an independent calculation of the same sums, not against a CAD program, and they say when a countersink, a slot or the treatment of the edges was approximated or left out. Saving has been tried on the file system of the machine that runs the tests only. The extrusions and the pockets have been tried on sketches made by the tests: the volume of one comes from the area of the outline in floating point and is rounded to a cubic nanometre (so the volume of a model that has one says it is not exact), a pocket is not checked against the holes and the posts it meets (where it meets a hole of the same face both volumes are counted), the sketch must lie on its face with no offset, and the STEP file and the model for fabrication refuse such a box while the script only says in a comment that it does not draw them. The domain `sketch` and the steps of versions 2 and 3 are written and read by this repository only; the board tool does not know them. The patterns and the cooling fins have been tried on boxes made by the tests. A pattern is limited by the count of the settings (`pattern.max_count`) and checked position by position, so the work grows with the square of the count; a circle uses the integer sine and cosine of the angles (nine decimals), so its positions are rounded to the nanometre and are the same on every system. The fins are checked as boxes against the square that holds each hole of their face (a round hole counts as its square), not against the extrusions and pockets of sketches; the STEP file refuses a box that has them, and the centre of mass and the moments leave them out and say so (the volume counts them). The estimate of the heat takes one coefficient for the whole surface, fins of constant section, no radiation and no interference between neighbouring fins; it is not compared with a measurement or with a simulation of the thermal lab, and its coefficient, conductivity and temperature are values of the settings that nobody has checked against a real heat sink. The catalog is the same: the libraries of the examples are made up, the shapes are a box, a cylinder, a cone cut off or a hexagonal prism (no thread, chamfer or radius), the volumes of round and hexagonal shapes use ratios of twelve decimals, the profile overlap check compares bounding boxes only (the interference check of the assemblies compares the shapes, and calls a screw that enters a profile a conflict), the cut list does not fit cuts into bars or allow for the kerf of the saw, the views draw the bounding box of each part, and the STEP file does not draw the parts.
