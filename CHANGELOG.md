# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here.

## [Unreleased]

- **The CI validation checks the workflows and the lessons of past CI failures against a baseline:** `tools/workflow_hygiene.py` and `tools/lessons_lint.py` (copies of the shared tools) run in the validation, and only findings that are not in `tools/workflow_hygiene_baseline.json` and `tools/lessons_lint_baseline.json` fail it, so what exists today stays as it is and a new one is caught. `python tools/workflow_hygiene.py --root . --detail` and `python tools/lessons_lint.py --repo .` list the findings.
- **The CI is split in two workflows:** the validation of the repository (manifest, documentation, pictures, tools) stays in `ci.yml` and the Rust jobs move to `rust.yml`, which does not run for a change that only touches Markdown files or pictures, so a change of the documentation no longer builds the code on two systems, and they keep a cache of the build. The names of the jobs are the same.
- **A pull request must say what it changes:** the validation runs `tools/changelog_pr_check.py` on a pull request (it fails when the CHANGELOG has no new line under [Unreleased]; the label `no-changelog` skips it), the same check as the other repositories of the suite.
- **The Rust toolchain is pinned:** `rust-toolchain.toml` fixes the version of Rust (the same one in every repository of the suite) with `rustfmt` and `clippy`, the workflows install that version and not `stable`, so a new release of Rust cannot turn the CI red by itself.
## [0.0.6] - Simple assemblies, mates and interference checks

- Add simple assemblies (`eh3d_phoenix_cad::assembly`): a mate places one part of the catalog relative to another along one axis, in whole nanometres, and takes away that one coordinate of the part and no other. There are three kinds: `gap` (the part lies on the side of the other that a direction names, `+x` to `-z`, with a distance of air between the faces), `align` (the lower faces, the upper faces or the middles of the two bounding boxes on one plane; the middles are refused when they differ by half a nanometre) and `stack` (the part stands on the other). The position typed for a part stays in the model and `Enclosure::placed_parts` applies the mates on top of it, so taking a mate away puts the part back; a chain of mates is solved from its free end whatever the order of the ids, and a part placed twice along one axis, a cycle, a part that is not in the model, a part joined to itself and a position outside the range of lengths are refused.
- Place mates as steps of the history (`Operation::AddMate` and `RemoveMate`, each the exact inverse of the other): a mate is checked together with the mates that are already there and with the limits of the catalog before it is kept, a refused one leaves the model equal to what it was, a part that a mate uses cannot be taken away until the mate is, and the printer model keeps the mates. Write them in the mechanical domain of the project file as the steps `add-mate` and `remove-mate`, which make version 5 of the domain; a recipe is written in the lowest version that can hold it, so every project without a mate keeps its version and its bytes, and the version and the steps have to agree in both directions.
- Find the parts that share volume (`assembly::interference`): the bounding boxes are sorted along `x` and compared, a pair that only touches is clear, and a pair whose boxes overlap is looked at again in the shapes, made of boxes (a solid profile, the four walls of a hollow one, the two legs of a bracket) and cylinders (the head and the shank of a screw), with integer arithmetic (squares of distances in doubled nanometres, so a centre on a half nanometre is exact); a box meets a cylinder and two cylinders along one axis exactly, and two cylinders along different axes as their boxes. A screw through the hole of a bracket is allowed when its shank lies inside the hole with the clearance of the settings and is counted as a pass. The report lists the pairs in conflict with the volume their bounding boxes share, how many pairs had overlapping boxes, how many are passes and how many are clear (a bar in the cavity of a tube, a round head near the corner of a bar).
- Add the codes `PHOENIX-CAD-046` (assembly not valid: a mate that cannot be solved or a part that a mate uses taken away) and `PHOENIX-CAD-047` (parts interfere), with their text in the eight languages (Portuguese of Portugal), and the settings `[assembly]` in `phoenix-cad.default.cfg` (`max_mates`, `hole_clearance` and `max_report_pairs`, each checked when the file is read) and `mate_kind`, `mate_direction`, `mate_distance`, `mate_axis` and `mate_edge` in `[new_step]` of `phoenix-cad-window.default.cfg`.
- Add the assembly to the window: a group of mates in the tree, a button that adds a mate between the selected part and the one before it (a stack, a gap or an alignment as the settings say), the fields of a mate (the two parts typed by name, the kind as a choice, the side and the distance of a gap or the axis and the faces of an alignment, and where the placed part really is), each change an entry of the history and each refusal told in the status bar with the mate left as it was, a field of a part that says where its mates put it and which parts it shares volume with, the parts in conflict drawn in their own colour in the 3D view and the plan (the selected part keeps the colour of the selection), and in the fit report the number of mates and the pairs in conflict or that there are none, all in the eight languages.
- Add the command `interference` to the program, which reads a project, puts the parts where their mates place them and prints the lines of the assembly (the number of mates and the pairs that share volume, in the language of `--lang`), exiting with 1 when a step was refused or two parts share volume; `check` prints the same lines at the end of its report when the project has two parts or more.
- Add an example assembly (`examples/assembly.phoenixproj`, version 5 of the domain): a base rail, two posts and a top rail placed with nine mates (stacks and alignments by the lower face, the upper face and the middle), a bracket against a post with a gap and an alignment, and a bolt through the hole of the bracket; and move the four bolts of the example frame into the holes of its brackets with their heads on the legs, where they had been 5 mm off the middle of the hole.
- Add 38 tests with values worked out by hand and checked again with a second calculation: every kind of mate in every direction (a gap of 5 mm from a part 100 mm tall at z = 7 mm puts the other at 112 mm, a stack at 107 mm, a gap on the side of smaller x at -18 mm, the middles at 37 mm), a chain solved against the order of its ids, the half nanometre of two middles, every refusal with its code, the golden text of the steps of version 5 and every spelling of a mate that is refused, the version in both directions, bounding boxes that touch and that overlap by 1 mm (3000 mm3) and by 1 nm, the walls of a hollow section of 40 mm with a bar 1 nm inside the wall, a profile turned onto another (30 x 20 x 20 mm shared), a screw through a hole of 4.5 mm that stops fitting 1 nm beyond 0.75 mm off the middle, the clearance of the settings, a round head that meets the corner of a bar at 1944543 nm and not at 1944544 nm, two screws whose heads touch at 5.5 mm, the sweep against all the pairs of forty random bars, and the window, the command and the examples.
- Bring the documentation up to date with the code: a page about the assemblies (`docs/ASSEMBLY.md`), the status, the scope, the ideas left for later (mates between faces and holes, joints that move, holes in a profile, the exact shared volume), the requirements (the new FCAD-008), the project domain (version 5 and its steps), the table of the codes, the catalog, the manifest, the eight README files (the description, the table of the status, the commands to try, the counters of the tests and the tree of files), the architecture and the repository structure.
- Write the solid of the model as an STL file (`eh3d_phoenix_cad::solid`, `mesh` and `triangulate`): the box and its inner space as two closed surfaces joined only by the walls of the holes; every hole in its five styles (the straight one, the countersink as a cone, the counterbore with its flat step, the rectangle and the slot); the posts with their foot and their bore; the centring lip; the extrusions and pockets of the sketches (a pocket through the face opens into the inner space and the islands of an outline stay); the cooling fins; the rounded or chamfered vertical edges; and every placed part of the catalog, in the place its mates give it, as a solid of its own (bars solid or hollow, screws with a cylindrical, countersunk or hexagonal head, brackets with their two holes). A flat region with holes in it is cut into triangles by cutting off ears after bridging each hole to the outline, never dropping a point of an outline, so that the edges of neighbouring pieces meet at the same vertices; the file is binary (80-byte header, the count, and for each triangle a normal and three corners as 32-bit floats in millimetres) or text with one `solid` for each body.
- Draw every circle of the solid with `mesh.circle_segments` sides and the area of the true circle, with a sine and a cosine made of sums and products only, so that the same model gives the same bytes on every computer and a plain hole, a post, a cone or a screw has exactly the volume of the model. Check every body before it is written: its surface is closed (every edge used once in each way, no triangle with a vertex twice) and the volume of its triangles, summed from signed tetrahedra, is the exact volume of the model (the holes of the mass module, the area of the outlines of the sketches, the volume of the catalog for each part) within `mesh.volume_tolerance_ppm`; the box of the example, with rounded corners, is about 10 parts per million away and the plain pieces about 10^-12.
- Refuse what the solid cannot be made of and say which shape and which other shape it meets: rounded or chamfered edges around the lid or the floor, a shape that is outside the thin part of its face or inside another shape, an extrusion, a fin or a pocket that touches the edge of its face, a post that meets a wall, and so on. Add the codes `PHOENIX-CAD-048` (shape the solid cannot be made of), `PHOENIX-CAD-049` (solid does not match the model: an open edge or a volume off by more than the settings allow) and `PHOENIX-CAD-050` (export above the limits: more triangles or entities than the settings allow), with their text in the eight languages (Portuguese of Portugal).
- Draw the plan, the front and the side as DXF and SVG files (`eh3d_phoenix_cad::drawing`): the outline of the box (with the arcs or the slanted lines of its treated corners in the plan) and the rectangles of what stands out of it on the layer `CONTORNO`, the openings of the holes and pockets of the face that the view looks at (a countersink or a counterbore as two circles, a slot as two lines and two arcs) on `AGUJEROS`, and the centre lines of the box and of every hole on `EJES`, in millimetres. The DXF file is R12 text with `LINE`, `CIRCLE` and `ARC` entities and `$INSUNITS` set to millimetres; the SVG file is scaled to the width of the page of the settings, with the width and the height of the box as dimension lines, the size of every hole as a label (`⌀` for a round one) and the scale in the corner. Both are the same text for the same model.
- Add the new formats to the Export menu of the window (the STL solid and the plan, the front and the side as DXF and as SVG) with the same box to write over a file that exists and the same checks of the target as the script and the STEP file, a binary file written whole, the name suggested from the project, and the sentences of the menu in the eight languages; and add the command `export PROJECT.phoenixproj --format stl|dxf|svg|step|script|report|bill [--view top|front|side] [--out FILE] [--overwrite] [--ascii|--binary]`, which never writes over a file unless `--overwrite` is given, prints what it wrote, and exits with 1 and the code of the reason when nothing was written. `export.format` of the settings of the window takes the new words.
- Add the settings `[mesh]` (`circle_segments`, `max_triangles`, `stl_binary`, `volume_tolerance_ppm`) and `[drawing]` (`max_entities`, `svg_width`, `dimensions`, `axis_overshoot`) to `phoenix-cad.default.cfg`, each checked on both sides of its range when the file is read (`PHOENIX-CAD-011`); the `config` command writes the shipped file, which is the one the tests read.

## [0.0.5] - Patterns, cooling fins, a library of catalogue parts, export from the window, drag and measure, and extrusion of sketches

- Repeat a hole or a post as one step of the history: a pattern (`eh3d_phoenix_cad::pattern::PatternOp`) is a seed, which is also the first position, and a layout, either a line (each position a step in `u` and `v` after the previous one, in `x` and `y` for a post) or a circle (the seed turned about a centre by an angle once more for each position, with the integer sine and cosine of the angles, so each position is rounded to the nanometre and is the same on every system). Each position becomes a hole or a post of its own, named `<name> 1`, `<name> 2`, ... under an id that follows from the id of the pattern, and is checked like one added by hand: the margins to the edges, the web between holes (the earlier positions of the same pattern among them), the walls, the other posts, the lid and the treatment of the edges. Either every position is added or none, the operation has an exact inverse, a change of the box that takes the room of a position away is refused, and a position is never changed alone: the pattern is edited as a whole and builds its positions again.
- Write the patterns in the mechanical domain of the project file as the steps `add-pattern` and `remove-pattern`, which make version 3 of the domain; a recipe is written in the lowest version that can hold it, so every project without the new steps keeps its bytes, and the version and the steps have to agree for the three versions. Add the code `PHOENIX-CAD-042` (pattern not valid: fewer than two positions, more than the settings allow, a step of nothing, a circle whose seed is at its centre, or a change to one position alone) with its text in the eight languages, and the limit of the settings `[pattern] max_count` (256 as shipped), which is checked before any position is worked out so that a hostile count does no work.
